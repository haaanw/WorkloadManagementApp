# Competitor Card: Strength And Coach Apps

Last updated: 2026-06-06

## Competitors

Strength logs and program apps:

- Strong
- Hevy
- Fitbod
- Boostcamp
- RP Hypertrophy
- Juggernaut AI
- Ladder
- Caliber

Coach and training platforms:

- TrainingPeaks
- Future
- TrainHeroic
- TrueCoach
- Everfit

## What This Cluster Sells

This cluster owns the actual training behavior: logging, templates, workouts, periodization, coach messaging, progression, PRs, and accountability.

## Current Signals

- Strong has a very large App Store footprint, simple logging, Apple Watch support, and Pro pricing around $4.99/month or $29.99/year, with lifetime options visible in the App Store.
- Hevy emphasizes free unlimited logging, social training, routines, custom exercises, measurements, graph history, and a low-friction gym log experience.
- Boostcamp Pro is $59.99/year or $14.99/month and combines a tracker with a large programs library and analytics.
- RP Hypertrophy App Store pricing shows $34.99/month, $199.99/6 months, and $299.99/year; it has a strong expert/science brand for hypertrophy.
- TrainingPeaks Premium is $134.99/year for athletes. Coach Edition starts at $21.99/month and adds premium athletes from $9/month/athlete.
- TrainingPeaks now promotes strength tools, including a Strength Builder with 1000+ exercise videos.
- Future is a high-price human coaching benchmark, commonly positioned around $149-$199/month.

## Why They Are Dangerous

If Tuwa asks users to log workouts, it competes with products that already own the logging muscle memory. If it asks coaches to manage athletes, it competes with platforms that already own coach workflows.

## Their Weaknesses

- Strong and Hevy are excellent logs but do not meaningfully combine HRV, sleep, RHR, recent load, soreness, and autoregulated session decisions.
- Fitbod and RP prescribe workouts but do not center Apple Watch recovery and HealthKit readiness.
- TrainingPeaks is powerful but endurance-first, complex, and often assumes a coach or high sports-science literacy.
- Future has accountability but is expensive and human-labor constrained.

## Tuwa Counterposition

Tuwa should not try to beat every strength app on program library size. It should win by being the adaptive layer:

> The log plus recovery-aware training review for self-coached strength and hybrid athletes.

## Messaging To Test

- "Strong logs the workout. Tuwa tells you how to adjust it."
- "For athletes outgrowing a gym notebook and a separate recovery app."
- "A weekly training review without hiring a coach."
- "Use your existing program. Tuwa adapts today's dose."

## Product Implications

- Importing or pasting programs is a key wedge because users already have programs.
- Repeated-session logging must be fast enough to not lose to Strong/Hevy.
- Weekly review and PDF/export can become a premium retention feature.
- Coach mode should start with small rosters and simple readiness flags, not enterprise complexity.
- Comparison pages should be honest: Strong/Hevy are better if the user only wants a log; Tuwa is better if they need recovery-aware adjustment.
