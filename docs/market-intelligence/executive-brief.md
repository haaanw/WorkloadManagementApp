# Executive Brief

Last updated: 2026-06-06

## The Answer

Tuwa should compete as an explainable adaptive training system for self-coached strength and hybrid athletes, not as a generic recovery app or broad AI health coach.

The market is big and moving in the right direction: fitness apps are growing, wearable tech is the top ACSM trend for 2026, and users increasingly expect AI and wearable data to become actionable guidance. But the market is also crowded. Bevel, Cora, Athlytic, WHOOP, Google Health, Garmin, Oura, TrainingPeaks, Strong, Hevy, Boostcamp, and RP all cover pieces of the value proposition.

The winning wedge is the "last mile" of training:

> Given my actual plan, recent workload, HRV, sleep, soreness, and RPE, what should I change in today's session?

## Where We Are

Tuwa has real product substance:

- Workout logging and templates.
- Recovery scoring from HealthKit.
- Workload tracking with EWMA/ACWR-style context.
- Autoregulation recommendations.
- PR detection and progress review.
- Coach-athlete flows.
- HealthKit privacy boundary where raw health data should stay on device.

But the current market is moving fast:

- Bevel now offers a broad free core with recovery, strain, strength, nutrition, and AI upgrade.
- Cora tells the all-in-one AI fitness coach story clearly.
- Athlytic remains a cheap Apple Watch recovery reference.
- WHOOP strengthened its lifting story with Strength Trainer and muscular load.
- Google Health/Fitbit is now a platform-level AI health coach story.
- Apple Watch already gives users Vitals and Training Load for free.

So the app cannot rely on "recovery plus load" as the main differentiation. That story is becoming category standard.

## The Competitor Vote

| Question | Vote |
|---|---|
| Are we competing with Athlytic? | Yes, but only if users see us as another score app. |
| Are we competing with Bevel and Cora? | Yes, especially on Apple Watch AI coaching. We need to be narrower and more serious. |
| Are we competing with WHOOP? | Yes, but as an Apple Watch/no-second-wearable alternative. Do not pretend WHOOP lacks strength anymore. |
| Are we competing with Strong/Hevy? | Yes, on logging habit. We need faster repeated-session logging or import. |
| Are we competing with TrainingPeaks? | Partly. We should avoid pure endurance/coached-athlete workflows at first. |
| Is Apple the existential threat? | Yes. Apple can make basic readiness free. We must own session adjustment and weekly review. |

## The Strategy Vote

Do:

- Own "adjust today's strength/hybrid session."
- Speak to serious self-coached athletes.
- Make recommendations transparent.
- Build weekly training review as a retention hook.
- Use privacy as a trust advantage.
- Use coach mode as distribution through small coaches.

Do not:

- Lead with ACWR.
- Claim injury prediction.
- Become a broad health chatbot.
- Try to beat Strong/Hevy only on logging.
- Try to beat TrainingPeaks for endurance coaches.
- Hide behind vague "AI insights" language.

## Recommended Positioning

Primary:

> Adaptive strength training from your recovery and workload.

Expanded:

> Tuwa connects your Apple Watch recovery signals, recent workload, workout log, RPE, soreness, and PR history so you know whether to push, cut volume, swap intensity, or recover.

## First Growth Bet

Build and promote a free "Apple Watch Strength Readiness Calculator."

Why:

- It creates a useful entry point without requiring an app install.
- It captures high-intent search around Apple Watch, HRV, recovery, and strength training.
- It demonstrates the product's core logic.
- It naturally converts to: "Want this automated from Apple Health and your workout log?"
