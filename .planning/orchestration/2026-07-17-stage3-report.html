My honest opinion

The grammar is finally coherent — but two tabs still read like an instrument panel that hasn't earned its calm.

What genuinely works

  • The verdict card is the product. "Recent court work loaded your legs — microdose before the match" in serif, over a strike-zone bar, with a real number adjustment — that one card justifies the whole Ink & Grain direction. Nothing else in the market looks or reasons like this.
  • The font fix matters more than any styling this week. We were art-directing a typeface that wasn't rendering. Now the voice you chose is actually the voice on screen — and the difference will compound across every future screen.
  • One grammar everywhere. Plates, hairlines, pills, zone rules — the import sheets, banners, profile and auth all speak it now. The app stopped looking like five different eras.

What I'd push on next (candid)

  1. The "Recommendation method updated" card is engineer voice on the hero screen. It's long, explains internals ("during this transition we show both..."), and visually competes with the readiness card directly above it. I'd cut it to one line with a disclosure — or sunset it entirely once the transition window passes.
  2. Content clips under the floating tab bar. On Home a teal CTA band peeks out behind the pill bar; Profile's "CONNECTED DEVICES" header is half-hidden. The bottom content margin needs one more step of clearance — small fix, disproportionate polish gain.
  3. The readiness number appears twice (Home hero "71", Recovery "71/100") with different framing. Defensible — one is a verdict, one is a breakdown — but the Recovery card's header + explainer paragraph is wordier than the v3 voice. Recovery is now the weakest-edited tab.
  4. Load tab is correct but flat. Three plates + two charts, no moment. It's the one tab with no serif, no accent, no hierarchy peak — reads like an admin page. A single emphasis treatment on ACWR (it's the app's core number!) would give it a spine.
  5. Tab icons are off-voice. A sun for Home, a pictogram runner for Log — stock metaphors under an editorial identity. Low priority, but a custom 5-glyph set would close the loop.
