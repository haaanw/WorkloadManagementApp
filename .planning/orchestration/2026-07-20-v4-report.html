My honest opinion

This one has an identity. The panel + needle is a real signature — but three leaks still say "iOS app" and one screen got left behind.

What genuinely works

  • The panel hero is ownable. No fitness app renders its core number as a black instrument panel with a swept needle. It photographs well, it demos well, and it makes the readiness number feel measured rather than gamified — which is exactly the product's claim.
  • One accent, used twice. The discipline of red-only-on-indices gives the whole app a physics. When something red moves, it means a reading changed. That's Braun thinking, actually executed.
  • The motion finally matches the material. Crisp mechanical settles instead of soft springs; the count-up needle sweep with detents is the one moment of theatre, and it's earned.

What I'd push next (candid)

  1. Sheet chrome is the biggest remaining leak. Open "Log Workout" — the most-used flow — and you're back in stock iOS: system-font sheet title, glass Cancel pill, the ~0.4s system sheet spring. The world-building breaks exactly where usage is highest. If the re-dogfood confirms the direction, this is the first follow-up lane I'd run.
  2. Recovery reads like the poor sibling. The Panel Law (one panel, Home owns it) leaves Recovery with no peak. Correct discipline, but the screen is now quiet to the point of plain. A future option: a micro tick-scale strip on the score card — instrument grammar without breaking the law.
  3. Chart palette clash. The Swift Charts still use the old olive/moss line colors, which sat well on warm paper but clash mildly with cool aluminum. Chart tokens were exempted from the sweep; a small retune would finish the room.
  4. Watch the small mono. 13pt Plex Mono in dense rows (Log history, metric rows) is information-rich but can tip into busy. Judge it on the device, not the sim.
  5. Detent haptics are unverified on hardware — the simulator has no Taptic engine. First thing to feel for on your phone: the click as the needle crosses into GO.
