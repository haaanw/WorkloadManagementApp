## 1. HAN's four topics, judged

The test: delete Tuwa from the finished article. Does it still stand?

| Topic | Stands without Tuwa? | Backbone | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barefoot shoes vs thick soles | Yes, entirely | none | **STRUCK** — already struck by name in the governing brief |
| High protein for energy and health | Yes, entirely | none | **REJECT as spoken** → converts, §2 |
| How much healthy fat | Yes, entirely | none | **REJECT as spoken** → folds into §2 |
| Circadian rhythm | Yes, mostly | weak | **CONVERT or drop**, §3 |

Two independent problems, and both must be fixed for anything here to ship.

**Problem 1 — no backbone.** Tuwa does not measure gait, does not sell shoes,
and does not track nutrition. All three articles stand with the product deleted.

**Problem 2 — the framings overclaim, independent of Rule 0.** Worth recording
because it survives whichever reading HAN picks:

- *"Thick insoles are ruining your body"* is not supportable. Minimal footwear
  does increase intrinsic foot muscle size over weeks (Ridge 2019; Miller 2014),
  and maximal cushioning did not reduce impact loading the way intuition predicts
  (Kulmala 2018) — but barefoot *transitions* injure people: bone-marrow oedema
  in transitioners (Ridge 2013), metatarsal stress-fracture series (Salzler).
  Barefoot is not safe-by-category. And chunky Crocs and height-increasing shoes
  have no literature at all — we would be borrowing conclusions from
  minimalist-running studies and hoping nobody checks.
- *"Protein is crucial for daily energy"* is wrong on its central claim. Protein
  is not a primary energy substrate. What is defensible: satiety per calorie
  (Weigle 2005), lean-mass preservation in a deficit (Longland 2016), ~1.6 g/kg/d
  for hypertrophy with the CI reaching ~2.2 (Morton 2018), per-meal distribution
  and the leucine threshold (Areta 2013; Mamerow 2014; Moore 2015), and no renal
  harm in healthy adults (Devries 2018).
- *"How much fat exactly"* has no exact answer. A floor, a ceiling, and a range
  conditioned on total energy. Refusing false precision is the honest position.

**Citation warning that binds every pointer above and below.** These are recall,
written to locate the literature. They are NOT verified citations. Editorial
rule 2 requires a named source read at first hand. Every pointer gets opened and
confirmed at draft time or it does not appear in an article. A planning doc must
never launder unverified citations into a published claim.

## 2. The one strong conversion — and it is worth writing

**"Why Tuwa never asks what you ate."**
Backbone type: **Decision**. Fits the founder voice, which is the class HAN
identified as the best fit for it.

Every competitor in the readiness category either bolts on nutrition tracking or
sells it as a tier. Tuwa refuses. That refusal is a real recorded product
decision, it is defensible on evidence, and the article cannot exist without the
product — which is exactly the test.

The protein and fat evidence becomes the *evidence for the decision* instead of
standing alone: the intake question has a defensible answer that is a range, not
a number; logging compliance decays fast; a wrong nutrition input would corrupt a
readiness verdict that currently refuses to guess. It carries the same
"What 'No Data' means" logic already accepted as a Mechanism topic.

This absorbs both the protein topic and the fat topic. **Write one article, not
two.** Two would split the argument and neither half would carry a backbone.

## 3. Circadian rhythm — convert or drop

The strongest of the four on search demand and the weakest on originality, once
Rule 0 applies.

The obvious backbone is already taken. "Why the morning median against your own
baseline, not last night's number" is starter topic 2 — **PASS, already
written**. A circadian article would restate it with more references.

The only non-duplicative conversion I can defend: **"What Tuwa refuses to infer
from the clock."** Decision class. The app knows session timestamps and does
nothing with time-of-day. Strength and power do trend toward a late-afternoon
peak tracking core temperature, chronotype shifts the window (Facer-Childs &
Brandstaetter 2015), and adaptation is partly time-of-day specific (Chtourou &
Souissi 2012) — yet none of that is stable enough per-athlete to move a verdict,
so we do not move it. Sleep **regularity** as an outcome in its own right
(Windred 2024) is the strongest under-exposed thread available.

**Claim rails bite hard here.** Sleep-score v2 is unshipped. The article names it
as non-shipping, exactly as the already-drafted sleep article does.

If HAN does not want that article, drop circadian. A weak backbone is worse than
no article, because it teaches the series that the gate is soft.
